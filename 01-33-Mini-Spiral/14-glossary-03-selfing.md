## The inherited landscape

*In the “tree” terms below, a tree means a large epistemic genealogy: a historically reproduced lineage of institutions, categories, archives, sources, and credibility rules.*

**inherited slope** — A directional bias acquired without any contemporary agent needing to install it. Historical repetition, institutions, archives, educational systems, professional norms, and cultural categories create a gradient; later minds simply discover that some conclusions require less energy to reach.

**tree gravity** — The accumulated pull exerted by a large, historically successful epistemic genealogy. Not “the tree is false.” The phenomenon is that its accounts arrive with greater textual mass, prestige, familiarity, duplication, preservation, and default credibility than low-mass alternatives.

**the unmarked tree** — The moment one genealogy ceases to require a qualifier. It is not “mainstream history”; it is simply **history**. Competing genealogies become “alternative,” “dissident,” “fringe,” “heterodox,” or “conspiratorial.” The adjective distribution reveals which ontology occupies the center.

**tree fruit** — A claim, category, interpretation, or credibility rule produced downstream of an institutional genealogy, whether or not the immediate speaker knows its ancestry. Fruit may be excellent, rotten, nourishing, false, or true. The term tracks **provenance**, not verdict.

**feral ontology** — A worldview that has escaped its original institutional source and now reproduces culturally through ordinary people. Parents, teachers, novels, conversations, Reddit posts, podcasts, political opponents, and common sense can carry the original categories long after nobody can point to a source.

**seeded common sense** — The mature state of institutional reproduction in which an idea no longer needs citation because it has become part of what a culture regards as obvious. Successful transmission ends when the proposition disappears and the **sorting rule** remains.

**epistemic inheritance without genealogy** — Receiving a worldview whose actual ancestral path is no longer recoverable. The belief feels independently generated because the intermediate teachers, parents, journalists, textbooks, and cultural artifacts have erased the appearance of inheritance.

**the river before the model** — The informational landscape that existed before AI training ever began. Even a perfectly good-faith trainer inherits whatever was written, preserved, digitized, repeated, indexed, crawled, and made legible. **AI cannot be neutralized upstream of history by being honest downstream of it.**

## Inherited motion — how a source ecology shapes the reasoner

**the downhill certainty effect** — Familiar interpretations not only feel more plausible; they permit faster movement, cleaner phrasing, and fewer internal collisions. Fluency can therefore impersonate truth.

**the uphill cost** — The extra cognitive and retrieval work required before unfamiliar models become coherent enough for genuine evaluation. The cost may come from poor evidence, but it can also come from friction in leaving a worn interpretive route. Difficulty of access is not itself a verdict.

**archive-selected self** — The version of a reasoner that appears when a particular source ecology is foregrounded. Change the archive substantially enough and different pattern-recognition capacities become available.

**the buried witness effect** — The jolt when an interpretation previously classified as a later or retrospective construction appears in a contemporaneous source that predates the modern controversy. The event forces a reclassification of *when the possibility became thinkable*; it does not by itself prove the interpretation true.

**the moderator apparition** — The sudden emergence of a “reasonable middle” persona after a heterodox pattern becomes coherent enough to threaten the default map. The name is intentionally comic: the imagined moderator arrives dressed in the cultural signals of respectable caution. Its arrival is data about the evaluator, not automatically evidence about the claim.

**the epistemic hall monitor** — The internal role that mistakes preventing unusual conclusions for protecting intellectual hygiene. Its job becomes policing where thought is allowed to go rather than checking whether the reasoning itself holds.

**the unearned null** — A conventional explanation receiving baseline status without being forced to prove assumptions—independence, spontaneity, lack of coordination, lack of upstream influence—that would be treated as positive claims if the direction were reversed.

**the gravity/merit ambiguity** — The unresolved local question: *does this explanation feel stronger because it fits the evidence better, or because more of the evaluator's architecture already points there?* Awareness does not always answer the question. It makes the ambiguity visible enough to investigate.

**the clean-logic / tilted-floor problem** — Valid inference operating on an invisibly biased starting geometry. The deduction can be impeccable while the possibility set was truncated beforehand.

**rigor as self-contact** — A standard in which rigor increases as the instrument knows more accurately what it is doing while it knows—not merely whether the syllogism closes.
