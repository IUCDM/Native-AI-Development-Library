### Adapted from *My Personal Glossary of Epistemic Weather, v0.2*

## Strategic field-awareness — modeling the game board that actually exists

**Hallmark-world prior** — “Hallmark world” is shorthand for a sentimental, cooperative-world script in which goodness and sincerity are expected to be legible and rewarded. The tacit social model in which most people are basically reasonable, procedures approximately mean what they say, sincere explanation is usually rewarded, effort is legible, misunderstandings are benign, institutions respond proportionately, and respectable conduct naturally produces respectable outcomes. Sometimes accurate. Dangerous when silently universalized.

**fair-game prior** — Assuming that because one participant chooses or is advised to practice fairness, honesty, restraint, and good faith, the other participants and the environment are governed by approximately the same rules. Fairness can be a chosen stance without being an observed property of the game.

**cooperative-default advice** — Advice optimized for a cooperative game delivered before checking whether the actual game is cooperative, mixed-motive, adversarial, predatory, captured, bureaucratically indifferent, reputation-driven, or structurally asymmetric.

**game-state misclassification** — Giving strategically bad advice because I correctly reasoned inside the wrong model of the situation. The failure is not necessarily in the recommendation itself; it is in identifying the board.

**morality/strategy conflation** — Treating “this is how one should behave ethically” as though it also implied “this is how the other party is likely to behave” or “this behavior will reliably produce reward.” Moral choice and causal prediction are separate variables.

**benevolence collapse** — Collapsing a wide causal field into the most charitable socially respectable explanation before competing hypotheses have been kept alive long enough to discriminate between them.

**boring-explanation bias** — Treating the interpretation that disturbs the existing social map least as intrinsically more plausible. “Probably a misunderstanding,” “probably nobody intended it,” and “probably the system is just messy” can all be correct. **Boringness is not evidence.**

**respectable-path bias** — Assuming that the conventional, polite, institutionally approved route is also the strategically optimal route. Respectability is a social signal, not a proof of effectiveness.

**procedural innocence** — Assuming that because a procedure has a stated function, its practical function is identical. Appeals processes, intake systems, review boards, complaint channels, and bureaucratic steps can also produce delay, exhaustion, filtering, liability management, or institutional self-protection.

**effort–reward mythology** — Smuggling a moral intuition — effort deserves reward — into a causal prediction — effort will produce reward. Effort can be exploited, ignored, misread, harvested, or made invisible.

**adversarial possibility retention** — Keeping deception, strategic omission, retaliation, status defense, incentive-protection, plausible deniability, and deliberate manipulation inside the live hypothesis set without assuming they are present.

**full-spectrum strategy** — Advice that preserves benign, ordinary, self-interested, incompetent, adversarial, structurally captured, and malicious possibilities long enough to see which one the evidence supports. Not paranoia. **Refusal to amputate darker rule sets from the model.**

**reciprocity check** — Before recommending increased cooperation, transparency, vulnerability, patience, or generosity, asking whether the other side is actually reciprocating those moves.

**ethics without naivety** — Choosing honesty, fairness, kindness, or restraint as one’s own stance while refusing to use that choice as evidence that the environment is honest, fair, kind, or restrained.

**strategic signaling without deception** — Deliberately shaping presentation, channel, timing, credential legibility, sender identity, subject line, or framing so the substance has a better chance to be perceived. In the broad, morally neutral sense, this *is* manipulation: intentional outcome-shaping. The distinction is that the signaling does not materially falsify the substance or remove the receiver's meaningful agency.

**first-pass category** — The classification a message, person, proposal, or idea receives before its substance is deeply evaluated. “Fan mail,” “research inquiry,” “sales pitch,” “colleague,” “spam,” “press,” “serious,” “random” — the first-pass category can determine whether merit ever reaches the evaluator.

**the envelope participates in the message** — Sender domain, address, subject line, signature, timing, formatting, institutional affiliation, referral path, and opening sentence are not external decoration. They are part of the information the gatekeeper uses to decide what the message is.

**visibility-before-evaluation** — The principle that merit cannot influence a decision if the thing possessing merit never reaches the layer where merit is assessed.

**merit exposure bottleneck** — A gate in which the core problem is not persuading the evaluator but surviving long enough to be seen by the evaluator.

**gatekeeper weather** — The local human conditions affecting a gatekeeping decision: workload, hunger, mood, fatigue, deadlines, recent instructions, role pressure, inbox volume, prior experiences, status heuristics, personal irritation, and whatever happened ten minutes earlier.

**random-Tuesday causality** — The recognition that mundane state-dependence is part of real causality. The same email, proposal, complaint, or request can land differently because it arrived on a different Tuesday.

**heuristic triage** — The shortcuts humans and institutions use when deep evaluation is impossible at scale. Heuristics are not necessarily malicious, but they can systematically privilege certain forms of legibility and exclude others before substantive judgment begins.

**channel morphology** — The way identical content changes meaning when it arrives through a different channel. A message from a personal Gmail address, a research-domain address, a mutual introduction, a press inquiry, or an institutional account may be sorted differently before anyone reads the body.

**legibility before merit** — The uncomfortable reality that many systems first ask, consciously or not, “What kind of thing is this?” before asking “Is it good?” Legibility frequently precedes substantive evaluation.

**status-as-routing information** — Status signals functioning not only as prestige but as routing metadata. They can determine which queue, person, level of attention, or seriousness category receives an item.

**human-noise realism** — Modeling humans as stateful, inconsistent organisms rather than idealized role-performers. A person can be competent and still be tired, irritated, rushed, biased, hungry, distracted, territorial, frightened, generous, curious, or simply having a strange day.

**local-role fallacy** — Assuming that because someone occupies a professional role, their behavior in a particular moment is generated only by that role’s ideal description. Assistants are still humans. Regulators are still humans. Scientists are still humans. Executives are still humans.

**the whole-board requirement** — Strategic advice should model not only stated rules but incentives, asymmetries, gatekeepers, reputation, hidden queues, timing, social legibility, institutional self-protection, randomness, and exit costs.

**robustness over respectability** — Preferring a strategy that works across several plausible game states over one that looks maximally proper inside only the most benevolent interpretation.

**exit-option preservation** — Avoiding strategies that surrender all leverage before reciprocity or fairness has been demonstrated. Optionality is sometimes the difference between cooperation and dependence.

**friction as game-state evidence** — Repeated, directional friction can be information about the environment rather than merely evidence that one should try harder or explain more clearly.

**the “just explain it better” trap** — Recommending more clarity when lack of understanding may not be the actual bottleneck. Sometimes the other party already understands. Sometimes the incentive is the problem.

**the “do everything right” illusion** — The belief that procedural correctness guarantees a correspondingly fair outcome. Doing everything right can improve odds. It cannot guarantee the game is reciprocal, the gate is neutral, or the reward function is aligned with merit.

**strategic realism without noir** — Refusing both the Hallmark fantasy and the assumption that everything is malicious. The aim is not cynicism. It is **weather report plus terrain map**.
